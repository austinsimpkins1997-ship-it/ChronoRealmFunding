Why support ChronoRealm?

When a team uses AI to assemble research, review records, or prepare a decision, the answer alone is not enough. A reviewer needs to see where a claim came from, what changed, what was tested, and what remains uncertain.

ChronoRealm is being developed around that need: a local evidence workspace that keeps source material, assertions, proposals, corrections, and authority boundaries distinguishable. Its organizing vocabulary—Source, Was, As, To Be, Becoming, Unknown, and Contested—supports an explicit distinction between what was recorded, what is proposed, and what has actually been established.

The reason to fund this milestone is concrete: help turn an existing prototype and inspectable evidence into a reproducible workflow that outside reviewers and pilot users can evaluate. The value proposition is clearer review and more traceable corrections. Improvements in time, accuracy, and usability still need to be measured.

Who could benefit?

The initial pilot would target researchers and small teams working with AI-assisted evidence packets. A representative task is to take a supplied source, prepare a claim and quotation, preserve attribution, review the proposal, and later inspect its correction history.

For example, when a source contains the same quotation twice, selecting the wrong occurrence can make a citation misleading. ChronoRealm's development work includes explicit occurrence selection and checks against malformed quotation choices. The proposed pilot tests whether those controls are understandable and useful to a human reviewer.

These are intended users and a proposed use case, not claims of existing customers or validated market demand.

What exists today?

  • A documented local software prototype with record/provenance workflows and an academic evidence preparation surface.
  • A public formal-model evidence repository containing recorded TLC outputs, reproduction material, claim boundaries, and falsification questions.
  • A local development checkpoint at v0.7.0-dev-22, documenting packaged checks and a bounded academic preparation milestone.

The September 13 development receipt states that native GUI/full live acceptance and installed activation remain outstanding. This brief summarizes project records; it does not claim a fresh independent rerun or production readiness.

Proposed eight-week milestone

The schedule begins after funding and scope agreement; reviewer and pilot availability may affect timing.

  1. Weeks 1–2: Freeze the baseline. Publish the bounded claims, synthetic task, reproduction instructions, and acceptance checklist.
  2. Weeks 3–5: Reproduce and challenge. Run available checks, engage external review within budget, and preserve failures and counterexamples. If no reviewer is secured, disclose the gap rather than relabel internal review as independent.
  3. Weeks 6–8: Exercise the human workflow. Attempt native/live acceptance on a named test environment, run the consenting pilot, and publish outcomes, limitations, and the next decision.

Success means an outsider can follow the published setup, inspect a claim's source and correction trail, and distinguish a proposed action from completed execution. The final report will record task completion, citation-selection errors, reviewer time, and observed failures. No performance improvement is promised in advance.
